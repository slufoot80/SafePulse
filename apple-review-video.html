Apple App Review Demo Video — What to Record

A step-by-step checklist for the demonstration video that proves SafePulse's automatic dead-man's-switch escalation works, end to end, so the reviewer approves the build.

The whole point: the reviewer cannot reproduce a time-based, server-driven escalation in their sandbox. The video has to prove the unbroken chain: the check-in timer runs out → the app sends automatically → the emergency contact actually receives the alert with the Maps link. Show both phones in one frame — the payoff happens on the second phone.

1 Pre-recording setup

  • Both phones unlocked, side by side in ONE frame — the SafePulse user phone and the emergency-contact phone. Unlocked means no fumbling to pick up or unlock during the shot. Open the Messages app on the second phone so the incoming SMS is visible the instant it lands.
  • Use the 30-minute check-in interval — it's a real production setting, so the reviewer has nothing odd to question. To avoid a 30-minute recording: press pause on the recording during the idle wait and resume for the final minute (see the recording script below).
  • Pre-configure the emergency contact beforehand — don't type a phone number on camera (privacy + speed).
  • Keep clocks visible: leave both phones' status-bar clocks and the app's countdown timer on screen so the elapsed time reads as real.
  • Plan narration or captions — reviewers skim; label every step on screen or in voiceover.

2 The recording script (in order)

  1. Open the app, show the main screen — establish that this is SafePulse, with the emergency contact already configured.
  2. Start recording, then arm the 30-minute check-in. Hold on the app for a few seconds so the timer clearly reads 30:00 at the start.
  3. Press pause on the recording and let the timer run down in real time. Both phones stay unlocked and in frame — nothing to touch.
  4. When about 1 minute is left, resume recording. Capture the on-screen countdown ticking down to 0:00 — this is the proof it fires automatically in real time.
  5. Show the SMS arriving on the second phone the instant the timer hits zero — from +1 888 423 5823, including the Maps link. Tap the link to show it opens the user's location.
  6. Keep rolling a few seconds after it arrives, then stop. Result is a ~2-minute raw clip: setup at 30:00 → final-minute countdown → automatic SMS + location.
  7. Close the loop — the contact was notified automatically, with a location link, with no action from the user.

3 How to actually record it

  • Film both unlocked phones with an external camera in ONE frame. A screen recording only captures one phone — it can't show the SMS landing on the second phone, which is the payoff. One camera on both phones solves this and removes any doubt about splicing.
  • Pause caveat: iOS's built-in screen recording can't pause — it only stops. An external camera can pause/resume, which is exactly why we use one. (If you must screen-record instead, capture two clips — setup + final minute — and send both.)
  • Keep the countdown timer and both status-bar clocks clearly readable the whole time — that's what proves the elapsed time is real.
  • 1080p, landscape, no music, no effects — send the raw clip.

4 How to submit it to Apple

  • In App Store Connect → your version → App Review Information → Notes, either attach the video file or paste an unlisted URL (unlisted YouTube or a direct link).
  • In the notes text, add a short paragraph: what the feature is, that it's server-side and can't be reproduced on demand, and that the recording was paused during the idle wait of the 30-minute interval — so the timer jumps from 30:00 to ~1:00, and the final minute is a continuous real-time capture of the automatic send.
  • Include a timestamped index so the reviewer jumps straight to the payoff:
0:00 30-minute check-in armed (timer shows 30:00)
· recording paused during the idle wait
0:20 recording resumes — ~1:00 left on the countdown
1:20 timer hits 0:00 → SMS + Maps link lands on the second phone
  • If Critical Alerts are shown but the entitlement isn't granted yet, say so so the reviewer isn't confused by an unapproved entitlement.

5 Common rejection triggers to avoid

  • Edited/spliced look → keep both phones in one continuous frame for the final minute; only the idle wait is paused.
  • Can't see the payoff → the contact's SMS + Maps link must be clearly legible on the second phone.
  • Dead air → pause the recording during the 30-minute wait; capture only the setup and the final minute.
  • Unexplained time jump → disclose in the notes that the recording was paused during the idle wait.
