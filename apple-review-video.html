Apple App Review Demo Video — What to Record

A shot-by-shot checklist for the demonstration video attached to the App Store submission. Its job is to clear the two rejection notes on submission 71af9035, and nothing else.

Rewritten 23 August 2026. This replaces the earlier two-phone, external-camera version — that plan is dead. One iPhone, screen recording only.

What this video must prove. Submission 71af9035 was rejected on 26 March 2026 on four notes: Guideline 2.1(b) — In-App Purchases not found (×2), and Guideline 5.1.2 / 5.1.2(iv) — contacts uploaded to a server without consent (×2). So the reviewer needs to see the consent handshake and the absence of any purchase screen. Alert delivery is the closing shot, not the point. Location is a footnote.
One phone is correct — and it is not a workaround. Add your own number as the emergency contact. The invite arrives on the same iPhone, you confirm on that phone, and the alert lands there too. This is the exact path the App Review Notes tell the reviewer to follow: “a US mobile number you control — your own is ideal.” No second phone, no camera, no tripod.

1 The three doubts the reviewer has

  • “Are contacts uploaded before anyone agrees?” → the written disclosure on the Add Contact screen, then Waiting for their consent, then the invite going out from the user’s own Messages composer.
  • “Did the recipient actually agree?” → tapping the one-time link, confirming, and the contact flipping to Approved.
  • “Is there a hidden paywall?” → a slow scroll through Settings and Home with no purchase, upgrade, or subscribe control anywhere.

2 Before you press record

  • Delete your own number from the contact list in the app first. If it is already Approved, Waiting for their consent never appears — and that frame is the entire 5.1.2 defence. This is the easiest way to waste a whole recording session.
  • Preflight the gateway. Send one real SOS and confirm the SMS and the email both arrive. A reviewer hitting a dead gateway reads it as a broken app.
  • Check the Twilio balance and the per-device spend cap. Number rental alone drains about $3.30 a month, and a 429 “cap reached” presents as an app that simply does not work.
  • Turn on a Focus mode so unrelated notification banners do not drop over the demo.
  • Record each scene as its own clip, named 01_ through 09_. Numbered clips remove the entire category of “the editor guessed the order”.

3 The nine clips

ClipWhat to record
01_setupLaunch, onboarding: enter a name and your callback number.
02_no_purchaseScroll Settings, then Home, slowly. No upgrade, subscribe, or unlock control anywhere. Hold long enough to read every row — this clip exists to prove a negative, so do not rush it.
03_disclosureContacts → Add contact. Hold four full seconds on the text stating that the contact’s name and number will be sent to the server. The most important frame in the video.
04_pendingEnter a name and your own mobile number, then save. Shows Waiting for their consent.
05_inviteTap Send invite → the iOS Messages composer opens pre-filled → tap Send. Show clearly that the user sends it, from their own phone.
06_consentThe invite text arrives → tap the link → confirm on the page that opens.
07_approvedBack in the app, the contact now reads Approved.
08_checkinHome → CHECK IN at the 30-minute interval. The countdown starts.
09_alertPress SOS → the alert arrives → open it, read it fully → tap the location link → the map opens.

Why SOS rather than a real 30-minute expiry: SOS fires immediately and travels the same gateway path, so there is no pausing, no time jump, and nothing for the reviewer to question. The old pause-and-resume trick is no longer needed.

4 Caption lines — send these with the clips

01 - Setup: user enters their name and a callback number. No account, no login. 02 - No purchases anywhere in the app. It is paid upfront, all features unlocked. 03 - Before a contact is saved, the app states that their name and number will be sent to our server. 04 - The contact is saved as "Waiting for their consent" and cannot be alerted yet. 05 - The invite is sent by the user, from their own Messages app. Our number never texts anyone first. 06 - The contact opts in themselves by confirming the one-time link. 07 - Only now is the contact approved and eligible to receive alerts. 08 - The user checks in. The countdown to the next check-in begins. 09 - Alert delivered to the approved contact, with the user's name, callback number and location link.

Send these as a numbered list matching the filenames, and state that the wording must be used verbatim. Editors rewriting captions into marketing voice is the most common way this job goes wrong.

5 What the editor does — and must not do

  • No seller can shoot this. It needs the real app, the real gateway, the real Twilio number and real contacts, and Apple requires the data to be genuine. The Fiverr role is editing only: trim, join in numbered order, burn in the captions, export.
  • Export vertical 1080×1920 MP4, about two minutes. The old landscape spec is superseded.
  • No intro card, no logo animation, no music, no stock footage, no transitions, no effects. Plain hard cuts. Boring is correct.
  • Never speed up, shorten, or re-time a clip to make the app look faster than it was. Apple is verifying real behaviour, and edited timing reads as staged.
  • Do not crop out the status bar or the clock.
  • A slight zoom is allowed on 03_disclosure and 06_consent only, so the small text is readable.

6 How to submit it to Apple

  • Upload Unlisted to YouTube, then paste the link into App Store Connect → your version → App Review Information → Notes, or attach the file directly.
  • The Notes text itself comes from APP_REVIEW_NOTES.md §1 in the iOS repo. That file is the single paste source — do not re-draft the notes here or anywhere else.
  • Include a short timestamped index so the reviewer can jump straight to the two clips that answer the rejection notes.
0:12 Settings and Home — no purchase control anywhere (2.1(b))
0:30 Disclosure shown before the contact is saved (5.1.2)
0:55 Invite sent by the user, from their own Messages app
1:15 Contact confirms, then flips to Approved
1:40 Alert delivered, with callback number and location link
  • If Critical Alerts behaviour appears on screen while the entitlement is still ungranted, say so in the notes so the reviewer is not confused by an unapproved entitlement.

7 Rejection triggers to avoid

  • Contact pre-configured off camera → the consent handshake becomes invisible and 5.1.2 goes unanswered. Add the contact on camera, every time. The previous version of this page advised the opposite; it was wrong.
  • Rushing the Settings scroll → if the reviewer cannot read every row, the absence of a paywall is not proven.
  • Music, intro cards or effects → makes a compliance video look like an advert, and invites doubt about what else was staged.
  • Any re-timing → unexplained time jumps read as splicing.
  • A non-US recipient number → non-+1 numbers route to WhatsApp, not SMS. Six re-shoots died watching the Messages app for something that was never going to arrive there.
