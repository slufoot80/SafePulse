Apple App Review Demo Video — What to Record

A step-by-step checklist for the demonstration video that proves SafePulse's locked-phone dead-man's-switch escalation works, end to end, so the reviewer approves the build.

The whole point: the reviewer cannot reproduce a time-based, server-driven, locked-phone escalation in their sandbox. The video has to prove the unbroken chain: user stops responding → server detects it → emergency contact actually receives the alert with the Maps link. Show both phones — the payoff happens off-device.

1 Pre-recording setup

  • Two phones in frame: the SafePulse user phone and the emergency-contact phone that receives the SMS.
  • Shorten the timer to a demo-friendly window (1–2 min) so the reviewer isn't watching dead air. If the interval is hardcoded long, use a debug/TestFlight build — and disclose it in the notes.
  • Pre-configure the emergency contact beforehand — don't type a phone number on camera (privacy + speed).
  • Keep clocks visible: leave the status-bar clocks on screen (or narrate timestamps) so it reads as real-time, not spliced.
  • Plan narration or captions — reviewers skim; label every step on screen or in voiceover.

2 The recording script (in order)

  1. Open the app, show the sign-in / main screen — establish that this is SafePulse.
  2. Show the emergency contact is configured (open the contact/settings screen briefly).
  3. Start a check-in / arm the timer — show the countdown or "active" state clearly.
  4. Lock the user's phone on camera (press the side button, show the lock screen). Don't cut away — continuity matters.
  5. Let the window elapse without checking in — this is the dead-man's-switch trigger. Narrate: "The user does not respond within the check-in window."
  6. Show the escalation firing on the locked phone — the Critical Alert / notification appearing on the lock screen.
  7. Pan to the emergency-contact phone and show the SMS arriving from +1 888 423 5823, including the Maps link. Tap the link to show it opens the user's location.
  8. Close the loop — state plainly: "The contact was notified with a location link even though the user's phone was locked and untouched."

3 How to actually record it

  • Do NOT rely solely on iOS screen recording. Control Center recording can't capture lock-screen alerts well and stops in some states. Film the physical phones with a second camera so the lock screen, the incoming Critical Alert, and the contact's SMS are all visibly real.
  • Split-screen / two-devices-in-one-frame is ideal — it removes any doubt about splicing.
  • Keep it short and unedited-looking (~1–3 min). One continuous take is the most convincing.
  • 1080p, landscape, no music, clear voiceover or captions.

4 How to submit it to Apple

  • In App Store Connect → your version → App Review Information → Notes, either attach the video file or paste an unlisted URL (unlisted YouTube or a direct link).
  • In the notes text, add a short paragraph: what the feature is, that it's server-side and can't be reproduced on demand, and that the demo build uses a shortened timer.
  • Include a timestamped index so the reviewer jumps straight to the payoff:
0:15 timer armed
0:45 phone locked
1:30 contact receives alert + Maps link
  • If Critical Alerts are shown but the entitlement isn't granted yet, say so so the reviewer isn't confused by an unapproved entitlement.

5 Common rejection triggers to avoid

  • Edited/spliced look → use one continuous take across both phones.
  • Can't see the payoff → the contact's SMS + Maps link must be clearly legible on camera.
  • Dead air → shorten the timer for the demo.
  • Unexplained test build → disclose the shortened interval in the notes.
